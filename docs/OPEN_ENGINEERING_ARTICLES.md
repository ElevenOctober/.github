# Open Engineering Articles

An **Open Engineering Article** records how a real problem was investigated and what the engineering work established. A shorter or narrower account may be titled an **Engineering Note**.

These are technical records, not claims of peer review or academic publication. They should be clear enough for another person to inspect the evidence and reproduce the relevant work.

## Article outline

Use the sections that apply. State when a section is not applicable rather than inventing detail.

### Problem

Describe the observed problem and distinguish evidence from assumptions.

### Origin

Include the provenance record: Problem ID, source type, original source, original URL, date identified, attribution preference, and verification. Protect private identities and restricted material.

### Why It Matters

Describe who is affected and the practical consequences.

### Existing Approaches

Summarize prior work and cite sources, licenses, and relevant limitations.

### Constraints

Record requirements, operating conditions, budget, power, size, privacy, safety, and other relevant limits.

### Investigation

Explain measurements, interviews, inspections, and reasoning. Include methods and equipment when they affect the result.

### Engineering

Describe key design decisions and trade-offs. Link the implementation and editable design sources.

### Prototype

Record the build, parts, software versions, configuration, and material changes.

### Testing

Give the procedure, conditions, equipment, units, and data needed to understand or repeat the evaluation.

### What Failed

Describe unsuccessful approaches, unexpected behavior, and known failure modes.

### Results

Report observations and measurements with enough context to interpret them. Do not invent or overstate results.

### Limitations

State what remains uncertain and the conditions under which the solution may not work.

### Reproduction

Provide exact steps, dependencies, required files, and safety notes.

### Contributors

Credit problem reporters, investigators, designers, implementers, reviewers, and testers according to their preference and consent.

### References

Link source material, upstream work, datasets, libraries, datasheets, and standards.

### Repository

Link the project repository and identify its license.

## Publication

Write collaboratively and preserve attribution. Separate measured results from interpretation. Include failures and negative results when they affect future work. Do not call an article peer-reviewed unless an independent review process actually occurred.
